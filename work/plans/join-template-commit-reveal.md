**stratagems ends up as a small addition on the template, not as a second app beside it.** It uses the template's systems (app context, connection, clock, submission and recovery, render seam, contracts), and where stratagems needs something the template lacks, that feature is built IN THE TEMPLATE TREE, at its home, and arrives by cascade, rather than being kept in stratagems. So every port step (6 onwards) begins with the `reconcile-template-tree` skill's home test: would a sibling game want this? If yes, it lands upstream first (in the template repo, following that repo's own work protocol, with the user's approval), and the stratagems step then consumes it. What stays in stratagems is its game: the rules, its contracts' game logic, its renderer, its brand.

Candidates already visible, each to be judged when its step comes (not decided here):

- **A client-side simulation of the contract** (stratagems' 700-line TypeScript reimplementation in `common/`; catacombs does the same with a local EVM): the template's plan notes both, so the seam for it may belong upstream.

## Decisions (confirmed by the user, 2026-09-30)

1. **Where stratagems' current app lives after the merge: `web/src/lib/stratagems/`**, moved there BEFORE the merge (step 2), so the template's `account/` and `ui/` land without interleaving, and so the port later dissolves one folder rather than untangling two. (reveal-or-die's game lives in `web/src/lib/world`; that name is taken by meaning, not by path.)
2. **The merge keeps stratagems' app shell** (its `+layout`, `+page`, `(pages)/`, routes) and the template's FRAMEWORK code and its unit tests, and omits the reference game (client and contracts) plus the template routes that need the template's app context. Those routes come back when the port adopts the app context (step 6). The alternative, taking the template's shell in the merge and mounting the game inside it, IS the port, and does not fit in one merge. **Confirmed, as a transition only**: the port then moves stratagems onto the template's system (see "The direction").
3. **Contracts toolchain versions: bump the template upstream first** (step U), so the merge's `contracts/package.json` and `rocketh/*` conflicts are about the game and not about versions. Without it, the merge takes stratagems' newer versions and every later cascade conflicts on them until the template catches up.
4. **The node's verify is the template's**: `pnpm install && pnpm --filter ./web check && pnpm --filter ./web run test:unit`, per the template's plan (check, not e2e). stratagems' own heavier checks (contracts tests, the alpha1 browser verification) stay stratagems' to run.
5. **The merge keeps stratagems' contracts; later, stratagems' contracts are rebuilt on the template's commit-reveal system.** At the merge the template's reference contracts, their deploy scripts and tests are omitted (they are a reference GAME today, and would deploy a second game beside stratagems'). Step 10 then happens in two parts: first, upstream, the template separates its commit-reveal mechanism into a game-agnostic base that a game extends with its own action type and resolution hook (the reference game becomes its first user); then stratagems un-omits that base and rebuilds its game logic on it. That is a new deployment; alpha1 stays as history.

### The port proper: order and measurements (specified 2026-10-24)

The order was decided with the user on 2026-10-24, from the measurements below (the log entry of that date has the method). Every step still starts with the home test from "The direction".

**What decided it.**

- **The contracts, mechanism by mechanism** (template-commit-reveal `main` 3fe2b29d, `contracts/src/game/internal/UsingGameInternal.sol`, against stratagems' alpha1 contracts). Both are routed (`*Commit`, `*Reveal`, `*Getters` routes), keep an ERC20 reserve, chain a commitment through `bytes24` heads one chunk at a time, forward a `payee` payment on commit and reveal (fuzd), and let anyone submit a reveal. What differs: the template's commitment carries a **bond** declared at commit time, the amount at risk for that turn; stratagems' carries none, and its stake is taken per placement at reveal (each placed gem is `NUM_TOKENS_PER_GEMS` held in the cell), with the whole reserve unwithdrawable while a commitment is open. The template has three cycle policies (`_cycle()`), a permissionless unanimous `advanceCycle`, attendance (who the cycle waits for), the `_playerOf` delegation seam with a `GameDelegation` route, a uint256 identity, a chunk size set at deployment and ONE hash encoding; stratagems has a timed `_epoch()` only, `msg.sender` identity, a constant chunk of 32 and TWO encodings (the last chunk drops `furtherMoves`). Forfeit: the template takes the bond in one call; stratagems walks the chain with the secret and burns per move, or lets the player burn their whole reserve. stratagems has things the template does not: EIP-2612 `permit`, a reveal paid from the wallet (`useReserve` false), `poke`, cells as ERC721, the rewards generator, a debug route.
- **The first commit-reveal rule is broken by alpha1, through one move the client never offers.** stratagems' resolution reads state another reveal of the same epoch writes (`epochWhenTokenIsAdded == epoch`, `_ownerOf(position) == player`, `life`). Those reads are not an optimisation: they ARE the collision rule (two placements on one cell in one epoch make it Evil, stake 2, both kept) and the owner's right to re-place or remove their own cell. Replayed in every order of three players over random epochs on a 3x3 board, with `common/`'s TypeScript copy of the contract (board, owners and reward points; token payouts are not in that model): **0 of 60,000** trials change with the order when every move is one the client allows (`web/src/lib/stratagems/actions/ActionHandler.ts`), and **659 of 3,000** do when every move the contract accepts is allowed. All of the difference comes from one class: a coloured move onto a cell another player owns, which the contract accepts and the client never builds (10,000 trials each: that class alone 2,358 differ; removing any cell, or colouring your own full-life cell, 0). What that move does, measured on one cell: B takes A's live cell for one gem, A's gem stays in the contract with nobody's name on it, and A KEEPS the reward points for the cell (the generator is never told), so points are counted twice. In one epoch where A re-places their own cell and B overwrites it, A-then-B gives B the cell and B-then-A makes it Evil with different points: whoever pays more gas decides. Refusing that move against the CURRENT board does not fix it (2,197 of 10,000 still differ), because it breaks the collision symmetry: the first of two placements on an empty cell would own it and the second be refused. The rule the user decided (2026-10-24) is judged against the board as it stood when the player committed, which no reveal of the same cycle can have changed yet. The scripts are `join-template-commit-reveal/order-check.ts` and `overwrite.ts` beside this file.

### Step 10a-contract: the commit-reveal base contract, in the template tree

In the template tree, so it follows `reconcile-template-tree` and each repo's own work protocol, and every push is asked for. **Decided** (the user, 2026-10-24 and 2026-10-25):

- **A base that games KEEP**, reversing "contracts are not inherited" for the commit-reveal mechanism: the reserve, the bonded commitment, cancellation, the chain of chunks (one encoding, chunk size set at deployment), the cycle policies, advance, attendance and the tally, delegation through `_playerOf`, the membership pair callable from custody, and both settlements, with the template's existing seams (`_playerOf`, `_forfeit`, `_cycle`).
- **Its shape: a Solidity `library` over ERC-7201 namespaced storage, with a convenience abstract contract beside it**, the shape of `@etherplay/delegation` (`Delegation.sol` and `UsingDelegation.sol`), whose library the template's `_playerOf` already composes. It lives IN the template, under its own folder `contracts/src/commit-reveal/`, not in a package: the package is postponed, and the code is written so that the move is a change of import. Composition is preferred, not a strict rule.
- **A game's actions stay typed (option T).** Solidity has no generics, so the game keeps its own typed routes (`reveal(player, Move[] calldata, ...)`), computes the one encoding `bytes24(keccak256(abi.encode(secret, actions, furtherActions)))`, and hands the base the digest and the number of actions; the base checks the chunk against the head, advances the chain, and charges the cost the game's resolution reports against the bond. The ABI and the indexers stay typed, and the reference game's client hash does not change.
- **The bond may exceed what the turn costs** (option b). The reveal takes each chunk's cost from the bond and releases the surplus with the last chunk (already built: `_reveal` sets `commitment.bond = 0` on the last chunk). A missed reveal settled WITH the chunks may be settled by anyone: it walks the chain, forfeits the game's penalty for what was committed (a hook; stratagems: one gem per move) and returns the surplus to the player. One settled WITHOUT them may be settled only by the player or their delegate, and loses the whole remaining bond. **Nobody but the player ever receives any of it**: what is forfeited goes to the game's `_forfeit` (burnt, kept, or a custody seized), never to whoever settles.
- **The penalty is the game's, and a custody stake still loses on both routes**: `with/nft-identity` bonds zero, so "only what was committed" would cost it nothing and let a player escape a bad reveal by settling with their own chunks; its `_forfeit` seizes the avatar whichever route settles.
